Standing scope-boundary ledger: 10 entries, so cross-wave defects stop being re-discovered

Adds docs/26-known-defects.md, the single standing record of every defect a wave
found but could not fix because it lived outside that wave's write scope.

Motivation is a measured pattern, not a felt one: waves 4, 5 and 6 each reported
problems "outside my scope", and docs/08's AMD coverage claim, the AMD cost gap,
the tools/index.py bare-id emitter and the stale MPT methodology note were each
named three times by three different agents. Nothing in the repo recorded that
they were known, so each naming cost a full re-derivation.

Contents: 10 entries (D1-D10) with what / where / which wave / why not then /
fixed now, plus Part 2 - what belongs in a ledger, what does not (a defect fixed
in the same wave that found it; anything restatable as a gotcha record; already-
ratcheting budgeted debt), and how an entry is retired (delete on verified fix,
keep-and-mark-disproven rather than delete, supersede into a gotcha).

Four figures in the commissioning brief did not survive measurement and are
recorded at their measured values with the discrepancy visible:

- D1: 18 `gaps` rows print "see EXCLUDED in tools/cost_per_token.py", but EXCLUDED
  holds 9 keys and contains NONE of the 18. Every one is a row on priced hardware
  whose real blocker is a missing GPU count. The message sends readers to the
  wrong dict. NEW finding, not in the brief.
- D2: 13 AMD Instinct throughput rows are priced but unpriceable, not 10. Of the
  13, 7 carry their GPU count verbatim and need only a BENCH_JOIN entry; 4 state
  it only in the record `name`, which check_evidence() cannot see; 2 are
  head-to-head rows naming two platforms and are correctly unpriceable. The 87-GPU
  row proves the divisor cannot be inferred from a sibling.
- D4: the index.py bare-id emitter does not exist. card() emits `{rid}` in
  backticks and the file contains no wikilink sequence at all; all 10 bare ids in
  00-index.md resolve to unique records, and build_site_data --check reports
  "docs 26, data problems 0". Recorded as disproven so it stops being re-reported.
  The real adjacent gap is that an unresolvable doc citation degrades to inline
  code silently - validate.py never reads docs/.
- D6: 17 nvidia-a100-sxm dangling refs, not 18. 30 total = 17 + gfx950(4) +
  gfx942(3) + mlx-quantized-4bit(3) + gfx1151(1) + 2bit(1) +
  amd-ryzen-strix-point(1).

D5 (stale MPT pointer), D7 (unused joules_per_token/memory_gb, verified zero
records each), D8 (MegaScale-Infer title divergence - and its notes still claim
